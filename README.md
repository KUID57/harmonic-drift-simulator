# 🎼 HarmonicField — Interval Ear Training Reimagined

Welcome to **HarmonicField**, a fresh take on interval ear training that transforms how musicians internalize the language of sound. Where traditional trainers stop at simple A/B comparisons, HarmonicField builds a **living soundscape** that adapts to your listening habits, fatigue levels, and genre preferences.

## 🧠 Why HarmonicField Exists

Most interval trainers treat your ears like a multiple-choice quiz machine. You hear a fifth, you guess a fifth, you move on. But music doesn’t work that way—intervals live inside melodies, chord progressions, and ambient noise. HarmonicField wraps interval recognition in **real musical contexts**, pulling sample phrases from classical, jazz, electronic, and folk traditions so that you're not just identifying abstract pitches—you’re recognizing the **emotional fingerprint** of each interval.

Think of it as a gym for your inner ear, but instead of lifting weights, you’re lifting **melodic fragments, harmonic colors, and rhythmic ornaments**.

## 🌱 Core Philosophy: Context-First Listening

The standard approach treats intervals as isolated events. HarmonicField believes intervals are **relational**. A perfect fourth sounds different when it arrives from below versus above, when it’s part of a descending bassline, or when it’s processed through a vintage synth filter. Our engine layers your training session with **ambient backing textures** (optional), allowing you to hear intervals the way you’d hear them on stage, in a studio, or through a practice amp—not in a sterile vacuum.

## 🎛️ Feature Matrix: What Makes HarmonicField Distinct

### 1. **Adaptive Difficulty Lattice** 🕸️
The trainer doesn’t just increase speed. It builds a multidimensional lattice of difficulty: interval size, tonal context, instrumentation, tempo, and reverb depth. Each session unlocks a unique combination, so you never plateau into routine.

### 2. **Genre-Aware Soundbank** 🎸
Choose from seven sound palettes—acoustic piano, nylon guitar, synthwave pad, orchestral strings, analog tape, pipe organ, and even a 8-bit chiptune mode. Each instrument brings unique overtones that challenge your ear differently. A major third on a pipe organ behaves nothing like one on a 8-bit square wave.

### 3. **Melodic Phrase Builder** 🎵
Instead of random intervals, HarmonicField inserts your target interval into a short, 3–5 note phrase. You hear the interval as part of a musical sentence, then you’re asked to identify it. This mimics real-world listening, where intervals appear mid-phrase, often with passing tones.

### 4. **Fatigue-Aware Session Engine** 🧠
Your ears get tired. Our algorithm tracks your response latency, accuracy trends, and even your headphone volume (via web audio API). When it detects listening fatigue, it shifts the session to **harmonic awareness mode**—slower, longer tones, with fewer test items—or suggests a break. It’s a coach that cares about your auditory health.

### 5. **Micro-Interval & Just Intonation Module** 🎼
Beyond standard equal temperament, HarmonicField includes a **microtonal lab** where you can compare quarter-tones, Pythagorean thirds, and septimal commas. This is a premium module for composers exploring non-Western scales or barbershop harmony.

### 6. **Progress Visualization Forest** 🌳
Track your improvement as a growing virtual forest. Each mastered interval category grows a unique tree species. Missed intervals produce saplings in need of care. It’s a visceral, non-numeric way to see your auditory landscape expand over months.

### 7. **Offline-First Architecture** 📡
All audio samples are pre-generated and bundled. The app works in airplane mode, in tunnels, or at that remote cabin without Wi-Fi. Your progress syncs when connectivity returns.

### 8. **Multilingual Interface** 🌍
The UI is available in English, Spanish, German, French, Japanese, and Portuguese. The audio itself is language-agnostic—intervals speak a universal tongue.

### 9. **Collaborative Ear Gym** 👥
Create or join practice circles. You can share custom phrase banks, challenge friends to “interval duels,” or contribute your own recorded instrument samples to the community soundbank (with moderation).

### 10. **24/7 Support & Community Forums** 🕓
Have a question at 3 AM about the difference between augmented fourth and diminished fifth? Our support docs are extensive, and the community forum hosts a dedicated section for audio theory Q&A. Monthly live Q&A sessions with ear-training specialists (rotating schedule, always announced in advance).

## 🌱 Getting Started (The Non-Technical Way)

1. **Open the Web App** — No account required for basic training. Session data stays on your device.
2. **Calibrate Your Voice** — Optional: Sing along with the intervals, and the app uses mic input to assess your pitch-matching skills.
3. **Choose Your Environment** — Select background texture (silence, cafe murmur, vinyl crackle, or ocean waves) and instrument palette.
4. **Start Your First Phrase** — You’ll hear a 3-note phrase. The target interval is embedded. Tap the interval you heard. The forest begins to grow.

## 🧪 The Science of Ear Training (A Brief Note)

Cognitive research suggests that distributed practice—short, frequent sessions—beats marathon listening. HarmonicField’s default session length is 7 minutes. The algorithm schedules micro-review intervals based on a spaced-repetition heuristic adapted from linguistics. You’ll be asked to recall intervals you haven’t heard in 2 days, then 5 days, then 12 days. This logarithmic spacing optimizes long-term retention.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a login?**
A: No. Open the HTML file or host it locally. Your progress lives in your browser’s storage.

**Q: Can I use this to train absolute pitch?**
A: No, Interval-Trainer is for relative pitch (the relationship between two notes). It does not help you identify a single pitch without external reference.

**Q: Why is the forest growing so slowly?**
A: The forest visual is intentionally recalibrated every 30 days. Early on, trees change quickly (each correct answer adds growth points). In months 2–3, the growth curve flattens, and an old tree may shed leaves when you miss a previously-mastered interval type. This mirrors real memory decay—use it to target your weak spots.
